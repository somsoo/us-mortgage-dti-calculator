Understanding Mortgage DTI Guidelines

1

Front-End Ratio

Calculates what portion of gross monthly income goes toward mortgage principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

2

Back-End Ratio

Combines your housing payment with all recurring consumer debts to evaluate total household leverage.

3

Approval Thresholds

While 36% is standard, automated Fannie Mae and FHA underwriting can approve up to 45% or 50% with good credit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between front-end and back-end DTI?

Front-end DTI measures only housing costs like mortgage, taxes, and insurance against income, while back-end DTI includes all recurring debts like credit cards and auto loans.

What is the maximum debt-to-income ratio to qualify for a mortgage?

Conventional mortgages typically require a maximum 43 to 45 percent back-end DTI, though FHA loans can accept up to 50 percent with strong compensating credit factors.

How can I quickly lower my DTI ratio before applying for a home loan?

You can lower your DTI by paying off high-interest credit card balances, consolidating student loans, avoiding new automobile leases, or adding a co-borrower.
